# Commercial licensing

x is dual-licensed.

**Option 1 — AGPL-3.0 (free).** See [`LICENSE`](./LICENSE). You may run, study,
modify and redistribute the software at no cost. The condition that matters:
under AGPL §13, if you run a modified version and let other people use it over a
network, you must offer those users the complete corresponding source of your
modified version.

**Option 2 — commercial licence (paid).** A separate agreement that removes the
AGPL's source-disclosure obligation.

## Which one do you need?

You do **not** need a commercial licence to:

- read the code, study it, or learn from it
- run it for yourself, your own team, or internally at your company
- modify it for internal use
- use it in teaching, research, or evaluation
- contribute changes back

You **do** need a commercial licence if you want to:

- offer this software, modified or not, as a hosted or managed service to third
  parties **without** publishing your source
- ship it inside a proprietary product you distribute
- build on it in a way that would otherwise put your own code under AGPL, and
  you cannot or will not release that code

The deciding question is not "am I a company" — it is **"am I willing to publish
my modifications?"** If yes, AGPL is free and sufficient, commercial use
included. If no, you need Option 2.

## Getting a licence

Open an issue at <https://github.com/GTMinions/x/issues> titled
`commercial licence enquiry`, or email **gtminions.dev@gmail.com**. Tell us roughly
what you are building and how you intend to deploy it, and we will send terms.

There is no published price list; terms depend on scale and use.

## Why it is set up this way

AGPL is a deliberate filter, not a trap. Anyone willing to work in the open pays
nothing, forever. The licence only becomes a cost for those who want to keep
their changes closed — and that is the case where a commercial relationship is
the honest arrangement.

## Contributors

Contributions are accepted under the [CLA](./CLA.md). Signing it is what makes
this dual licence possible: without the rights it grants, contributed code could
not be included in a commercially-licensed build. Your contribution stays
AGPL-licensed to the public regardless.
